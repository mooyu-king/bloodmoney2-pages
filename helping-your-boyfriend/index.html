About Helping Your Boyfriend

Helping Your Boyfriend is a horror romance visual novel by Zieelane and Mousefishdog. Its starting relationship is already established: Adrian is your doctor boyfriend, and you live together while a condition affecting your legs prevents you from walking. You wake during the night, help him take apart old dolls, and encounter a stranger who threatens you and forces you to take a pill. Those details create a story about proximity and uncertainty rather than a comfortable romance in which affection reliably signals safety.

This guide approaches the game through the distribution of practical power. Who can move freely, who supplies explanations, and who decides what counts as necessary assistance? These are reading questions, not hidden statistics or a claim that every action changes an ending. The value of the premise is that an ordinary domestic arrangement can hold several meanings at once. Help can be genuinely useful while the relationship surrounding it remains frightening. The protagonist deserves autonomy regardless of how much assistance they require.

The player above embeds a separately hosted browser build, so a working network connection and an available host are needed to load it. Features may differ between versions. Official feature descriptions are therefore separated from browser compatibility claims throughout this guide. Treat the supplied screenshots as visual references, not a promise that every scene or feature is reachable in the embedded version.

Before You Start: Set a Comfortable Boundary

Read the warnings before pressing Play. Helping Your Boyfriend includes violence, blood and gore, flashing imagery, jumpscares, and toxic relationship themes. Its hearts, warm colors and romantic title are not a promise of gentle content. If medical threats, coercive intimacy or vulnerability within a home are subjects you prefer to avoid, there is no requirement to continue for the sake of completion. A synopsis cannot predict every personal reaction, so choose your session with your own limits in mind.

A calm first session is more useful than a fast one. Keep sound at a comfortable level, make the text large enough to read, and avoid treating fullscreen as compulsory. Fullscreen may make visual detail clearer, but it can also make an abrupt image more intense. Pause when you need to. Reducing sound does not remove flashing effects or disturbing pictures, and this guide cannot promise that the build includes a setting that disables them.

Choose whether you want a mostly unspoiled first reading or a more analytical replay. On the first pass, a short note about a question is enough. You do not need to record every line or predict a perfect ending before the relationship has had time to establish its emotional rhythm.

Adrian and the Authority of Reassurance

Adrian combines two roles that would normally provide reassurance: a romantic partner and a doctor. The overlap makes his words especially important to examine. An explanation can sound authoritative because of a profession, affectionate because of a relationship, or both at once. Neither tone alone tells the player whether the protagonist has enough information to make an independent decision. Notice what a statement actually explains before deciding what emotional response its delivery seems to request.

A useful reading practice is to separate observation, interpretation and uncertainty. An observation records something the scene explicitly presents. An interpretation suggests what it might mean. Uncertainty marks what the protagonist or player does not yet know. Keeping those categories distinct prevents an early suspicion from becoming an invented fact. It also preserves the pleasure of discovering that a later conversation changes the meaning of an earlier detail without pretending the first impression was foolish.

The artwork can contribute to this ambiguity without providing a solution. A smile, a blush or a close portrait can invite affection; a profession and a familiar room can invite trust. Read them alongside the dialogue rather than treating any single expression as a reliable lie detector or a secret route instruction.

Dependence Is Not the Same as Consent

The protagonist cannot walk because of a leg condition. That is an important material constraint, not a reason to describe them as less capable of judgment or less entitled to privacy. A thoughtful reading distinguishes needing assistance from agreeing to whatever another person wants. The horror is not that a person has a disability. It emerges from threats, uncertainty and the possibility that access to care may become a source of leverage within an intimate relationship.

When considering a scene, ask which alternatives the protagonist can realistically perceive. A reassuring answer may feel different when the speaker also controls access to information or practical help. This does not require inventing an escape route, diagnosing a character, or guessing medical facts that the game has not supplied. It means recognizing the difference between a choice that sounds voluntary and a situation that places pressure around that choice.

This lens also helps avoid blaming the protagonist for not behaving like an outside observer. The player can pause and reflect with distance; a character inside the situation has limited knowledge and immediate needs. Reading that gap carefully often reveals more than judging each response as simply clever or foolish.

Dolls, Domestic Tasks and Participation

Helping Adrian dismantle old dolls gives the title a concrete activity. A request to help is not only something to agree with in dialogue; it becomes a task that asks for attention and physical input. That movement between conversation and action is worth noticing. A player may understand a scene intellectually while the repeated motions of a minigame make the request feel routine, absorbing or uncomfortable in a different way.

Do not assume the objects have a specific hidden origin or that the task proves an unconfirmed theory. The supplied premise establishes old dolls and dismantling, but it does not authorize a complete explanation of what they mean. Instead, compare the framing of the work with how doing it feels. Is the explanation clear? Does the mood encourage you to hurry? Are you concentrating so closely on the immediate motion that you stop considering the request around it?

The official description lists five minigames. That is an official project feature count, not a verified inventory of this hosted browser build. Read each activity prompt when it appears and avoid transferring the rules of one activity to the next. Success at an input task and agreement with a character are different questions.

Controls: Separate Reading From Reaction

The supplied controls use a click or Enter to advance dialogue, mouse dragging for minigames, and Space to hide the dialogue display. L opens the dialogue history, A enables automatic progression, and Ctrl or Tab skips text. Quick-time prompts use the arrow keys with Space. The bell at the top provides access to saving, loading and settings. These are reference controls, not a claim that each shortcut was tested in this embedded edition; follow its visible instructions if they differ.

Space deserves particular attention because its purpose depends on context. Hiding dialogue while reading is not the same action as answering a quick-time prompt. Read the current screen before pressing a remembered key repeatedly. Likewise, skipping is useful for material you already recognize, but it can make an unfamiliar scene harder to follow. Disable automatic progression when you want to inspect expressions or compare a line with the history.

Click inside the game frame before using keyboard controls. A browser page and an embedded game do not always share keyboard focus. If the outer page scrolls or a browser shortcut responds, that is not evidence that the story requires a different decision. Refocus the frame and consult the displayed prompt rather than sending a burst of inputs.

The Nighttime Threat and Uncertain Information

The premise includes waking at night and encountering a stranger who threatens the protagonist and forces them to take a pill. A stranger is not automatically a trustworthy alternative to a familiar person, and familiarity is not automatically proof of safety. This tension prevents the story from being reduced to a simple instruction to believe whichever person speaks last. Pay attention to what each person knows, what they demand, and whether they allow questions.

Medication belongs to the fictional threat in this description. Nothing here is medical advice, a diagnosis of the protagonist, or guidance for handling real medication. For literary analysis, the important question is how a supposedly protective action is presented when the recipient is frightened or unable to refuse freely. The difference between an explanation and an order can carry considerable weight even when the same object appears in both.

On replay, revisit the earliest moment when your interpretation changed. Identify the actual new information rather than simply noting that the mood became darker. That practice lets you compare routes without constructing an unsupported chain of motives for characters whose intentions may still be unresolved.

Four Endings Without an Invented Walkthrough

The official project description advertises four endings and supports customization of the protagonist name, pronouns and body type. Those details describe the official project, not a guarantee that this browser host contains the same release or every advertised option. Check the current menu before planning a completion run. Customization can change how personally a scene reads, but this guide does not claim that a name, pronoun or body selection unlocks a particular outcome.

For replay, use a small record of decisions and observations rather than an exhaustive answer sheet. Note the save you began from, the choice you changed, and the next scene that differed. Keep everything else stable where possible. If several actions change together, an ending comparison cannot establish which action mattered. Treat a pattern as a hypothesis until another run actually supports it.

No ending names, exact unlock sequences or guaranteed best choices are supplied here because they have not been verified. An ending can be discussed through emotional consequence, retained autonomy and newly revealed information without ranking it as a prize. Reaching an uncomfortable outcome does not mean you have failed to understand the story.

Browser Limits and Responsible Troubleshooting

The game is loaded from a different host after you choose to start. A blank frame can reflect slow asset delivery, a blocked request, a browser limitation or a host policy rather than a problem with your choices. Wait for visible loading to finish before refreshing. If it remains unavailable, record the device, browser and any visible message. An authorization error may need investigation by the host rather than repeated clicking or changes to your saved progress.

Desktop input is the most direct match for mouse dragging and keyboard prompts, but no device compatibility claim has been established by testing here. On a phone, rotating the screen may improve space without providing missing keyboard controls. Do not assume a touch gesture replaces every arrow-key sequence. If the build does not expose the needed controls, a keyboard-equipped device may be necessary.

Use the bell menu to check save and load options before a long session. Persistence across refreshes, private browsing, different devices or cleared site data is not guaranteed. Avoid deleting browser storage as a first troubleshooting step because it may remove progress. Keep your own short notes, and treat the two videos below as optional viewing that may reveal story material rather than a verified walkthrough.
